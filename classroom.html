2 What to send in

Two of these are on no supply list The change of clothes and the white T-shirt come only from this letter — they aren't on the school's supply list, so they're easy to miss.

  • Indoor shoes to stay at school Crocs or similar with rubber soles work well. No sandals or flip-flops. Must fit securely and stay on his feet. Keeps the classroom cleaner.
  • A complete change of clothes, in a Ziplock bag labelled with his name Her words: accidents happen, especially while little ones adjust to a new environment.
  • One white cotton T-shirt with room to grow, in a labelled Ziplock bag She tie-dyes these for the children to wear on field trips. They stay at school and come home after the Step-Up Celebration.
  • An afternoon snack, every single day Healthy, easy to eat, easy to clean up. He can also finish something from a packed lunch. "Kinders are very sad when they are snackless."

Label everything

Her emphasis, not mine — water bottles, shoes, lunchboxes, jackets, sweaters, and anything else he brings. She suggests writing on clothing tags, a clothing stamp, or iron-on labels, and warns about the size of the lost-and-found by June.

The first day is different

Kindergarten families come in with their children, help them settle, hear a few words from Mrs. Colletti, then a last hug and out — and the Boo-Hoo/Yahoo breakfast follows immediately. So on Aug 24 you go inside; from Aug 25 it's the door.

Dismissal — the check-out routine

Before leaving, every child has to look at her, say goodbye, and point to the person collecting them. If he runs straight to you without checking out, she asks that you send him back to finish the routine.

Worth practising with him — it's a safety procedure she's explicit about, and being the parent who waves him back rather than scooping him up helps her.

Absences and early pickup

Tell her if he'll be absent, leaving early, or being collected by someone different. Email ahead where you can.

To collect him before 3:45, go to the Modular Office first to complete the early-dismissal process.

6 Good to know

Homework

No regular maths homework expected. Occasional special projects, always with advance notice. Her one ask: read together every day — in English, Spanish, or whichever language you use at home.

Birthdays 🎂

She invites a parent or special person in to read a book to the class on or near the birthday — contact her one or two weeks ahead to arrange it. Summer birthdays are celebrated before the Step-Up Celebration, so nobody misses out.

The class pet 🐰

A stuffed bunny, named by the class. Children take turns bringing it home for an adventure — send photos, or add them to the class Google Slides.

Toys and stuffed animals

Allowed at his own risk. They stay in his backpack during lessons, can come out at recess, and go back after lunch. Her warning: kindergarten has many tiny hands — anything precious or irreplaceable is safest left at home.

Forms

All required CPS forms should be completed and returned. The Parent Questionnaire and Student Information forms are due within the first two weeks of school.
